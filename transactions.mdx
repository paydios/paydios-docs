Every payment, refund, and failed attempt is recorded as a transaction in PkPay+. The **Transactions** section of your merchant dashboard gives you a complete, searchable history of all activity on your account — from the first payment you accepted to the most recent refund you issued. Use it to investigate payment issues, track revenue, manage refunds, and prepare reconciliation exports.

## Transaction statuses

Each transaction carries a status that reflects its current state in the payment lifecycle.

| Status | Description |
|---|---|
| `paid` | Payment completed successfully. Funds are settled or pending settlement to your account. |
| `pending` | Payment has been initiated and is awaiting confirmation from the card network or payment provider. |
| `failed` | The payment attempt was declined by the card issuer or failed due to a processing error. The customer was not charged. |
| `refunded` | A full or partial refund has been issued to the customer. |
| `disputed` | The customer has raised a chargeback or dispute with their bank. See [Disputes](/disputes) for how to respond. |

## Filtering transactions

Use the filter controls at the top of the Transactions list to narrow your view. Available filters include:

- **Date range** — select a start and end date to view transactions in a specific period
- **Status** — filter by one or more statuses (e.g. show only `failed` or `disputed` transactions)
- **Payment method** — filter by card type, wallet, or payment rail (e.g. Visa, Apple Pay, Bitcoin, PIX)
- **Amount range** — set minimum and maximum transaction amounts
- **Currency** — filter by any of the 135+ supported currencies
- **Customer name or email** — search for all transactions linked to a specific customer

You can combine multiple filters at once. The list updates in real time as you adjust your criteria.

## Transaction details

Click any transaction in the list to open its full detail view. The detail page shows:

- **Amount and currency** — the exact charge amount in the transaction currency
- **Payment method** — card brand, last four digits, or wallet / payment rail used
- **Customer information** — name, email, and billing address
- **Timestamp** — date and time the transaction was created and last updated
- **Session ID** — the checkout session identifier, useful for debugging API integrations
- **Raw response data** — the full response from the payment processor, including authorization codes and decline reasons for failed payments

## Issuing a refund

You can issue a full or partial refund on any `paid` transaction directly from the dashboard.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the transaction">
    In the **Transactions** list, click the transaction you want to refund to open its detail page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Refund">
    Select the **Refund** button on the transaction detail page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the refund amount">
    Select **Full refund** to return the entire transaction amount, or enter a specific amount for a **partial refund**. Partial refunds can be issued multiple times up to the original transaction total.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the refund">
    Review the refund details and confirm. The refund is processed immediately. Funds typically appear in the customer's account within **5–10 business days**, depending on their card issuer or payment provider.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Issuing a refund does not automatically cancel an open dispute. If the customer has already raised a chargeback, the refund and the dispute are handled as separate processes. See [Disputes](/disputes) for more information.
</Note>

## Exporting transactions

To export your transaction data for accounting or reconciliation, apply the filters you need to narrow the result set, then click **Export**. PkPay+ generates a CSV file containing all matching transaction records, including amounts, statuses, payment methods, customer details, and timestamps.

Export the filtered set rather than the full history to keep files manageable and directly relevant to the period or report you're preparing.

<Tip>
  Use transaction exports alongside your payout reports for end-of-month reconciliation. Filter transactions by the same date range as your payout period to match settled amounts against the funds received in your bank account.
</Tip>
